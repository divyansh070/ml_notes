# PART 24 — POSITIVE DEFINITE & POSITIVE SEMIDEFINITE MATRICES

Positive Definite (PD) and Positive Semidefinite (PSD) matrices are the matrix equivalents of positive and non-negative numbers. They appear everywhere in data science: covariance matrices, loss function Hessians, distance metrics, and kernel Gram matrices.

---

## 24.1 Quadratic Forms

A **Quadratic Form** is a scalar function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ where every term has degree 2, generated by a symmetric matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$:

$$
f(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} A_{ij} x_i x_j
$$

### Hand Calculation Example ($2 \times 2$)
Let $\mathbf{x} = [x_1, x_2]^T$ and:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}
$$


$$
\mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x} =
\begin{bmatrix}
x_1 & x_2
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 1 \\
1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
x_1 \\ x_2
\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}
x_1 & x_2
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
2x_1 + x_2 \\
x_1 + 2x_2
\end{bmatrix} = 2x_1^2 + 2x_1 x_2 + 2x_2^2
$$

## 24.2 Definitions and Geometric Classification

```
     POSITIVE DEFINITE (Bowl)               INDEFINITE (Saddle Point)
               z                                       z
               │      (Min at origin)                  │        (Up in x, Down in y)
               │    )                          ╭───────┼───────╮
               │   (                           │       │       │
               └───┴──────► x                  └───●───┴───────┴──► x
                  y                                (Saddle)
```

| Class | Notation | Condition on $\mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x}$ | Eigenvalues | Geometric Shape | Optimization Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Positive Definite** | $A \succ 0$ | $\mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x} > 0 \quad (\forall \mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{0})$ | All $\lambda_i > 0$ | Upward bowl | Strict local/global minimum |
| **Positive Semidefinite** | $A \succeq 0$ | $\mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x} \ge 0 \quad (\forall \mathbf{x})$ | All $\lambda_i \ge 0$ | Flat-bottomed valley | Flat minimum / non-unique |
| **Negative Definite** | $A \prec 0$ | $\mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x} < 0 \quad (\forall \mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{0})$ | All $\lambda_i < 0$ | Downward dome | Strict local/global maximum |
| **Indefinite** | — | Positive for some $\mathbf{x}$, negative for others | Mixed positive & negative | **Saddle point** | Saddle point (escape direction exists) |

## 24.3 Why Covariance Matrices Are Always PSD

Let $X_c \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ be a mean-centered data matrix. The sample covariance matrix is:

$$
\Sigma = \frac{1}{n-1} X_c^T X_c
$$

### Hand Proof:
For any arbitrary non-zero vector $\mathbf{u} \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

$$
\mathbf{u}^T \Sigma \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{u}^T \left(\frac{1}{n-1} X_c^T X_c\right) \mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{n-1} (X_c \mathbf{u})^T (X_c \mathbf{u}) = \frac{1}{n-1} \|X_c \mathbf{u}\|_2^2
$$

Because the squared Euclidean norm of any vector is always non-negative ($\|X_c \mathbf{u}\|_2^2 \ge 0$):

$$
\mathbf{u}^T \Sigma \mathbf{u} \ge 0 \implies \Sigma \succeq 0 \quad \blacksquare
$$

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Data Science Meaning:** The variance of data projected onto any direction $\mathbf{u}$ is $\text{Var}(X_c \mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{u}^T \Sigma \mathbf{u}$. Because variance can never be negative, every covariance matrix must be Positive Semidefinite.

## 24.4 Hessian Matrix and Convex Optimization

In multivariate optimization, the curvature of the loss surface $\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w})$ is governed by the **Hessian matrix** $H = \nabla^2 \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w})$:

1. **Strict Convexity ($H \succ 0$):** If the Hessian is Positive Definite everywhere, the loss function is strictly convex (a bowl), guaranteeing a **unique global minimum**.
2. **Saddle Points ($H$ Indefinite):** If $H$ has both positive and negative eigenvalues, the stationary point is a saddle point. First-order and second-order optimizers must escape along the negative curvature eigenvectors.

## 24.5 Gilbert Strang's 5 Equivalent Tests for $A \succ 0$

For any real symmetric matrix $A = A^T \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, the following 5 conditions are mathematically equivalent:

```
                    5 EQUIVALENT TESTS FOR POSITIVE DEFINITENESS
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 1. Quadratic Form Test:  x^T A x > 0 for all non-zero vectors x ≠ 0    │
   │ 2. Eigenvalue Test:      All eigenvalues λ_1, ..., λ_n > 0 strictly    │
   │ 3. Sylvester's Minors:   All n leading principal sub-determinants > 0  │
   │ 4. Pivot Test:           All n pivots in Gaussian elimination > 0      │
   │ 5. Cholesky Factor:      A = L L^T where L is lower triangular with   │
   │                          strictly positive diagonal entries (l_ii > 0) │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Hand Verification Example:
Test:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}
$$

* **Sylvester's Minors Test:**
  * 1st Leading Principal Minor: $\det([4]) = 4 > 0 \quad \checkmark$
  * 2nd Leading Principal Minor: $\det(A) = (4)(3) - (2)(2) = 12 - 4 = 8 > 0 \quad \checkmark$
* *Conclusion: $A$ is strictly Positive Definite ($A \succ 0$)*.

## 24.6 Advanced / Optional — Do not study until Core track is complete

### 1. Rayleigh Quotient & Rayleigh-Ritz Theorem
For a symmetric matrix $A$, the **Rayleigh Quotient** is:

$$
R_A(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{x}} \quad (\mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{0})
$$

The Rayleigh-Ritz Theorem establishes bounds:

$$
\lambda_{\min}(A) \le \frac{\mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^T \mathbf{x}} \le \lambda_{\max}(A)
$$

Maximizing $R_\Sigma(\mathbf{w})$ under $\|\mathbf{w}\|_2 = 1$ is the foundational derivation of PCA.

### 2. Mercer's Theorem & Kernel Gram Matrices
In Support Vector Machines (SVMs), a kernel function $k(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}')$ defines a valid inner product in a Hilbert space if and only if every Gram matrix $K_{ij} = k(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j)$ is **Positive Semidefinite ($K \succeq 0$)**. This guarantees that the dual optimization problem is convex with a unique global minimum.

### 3. Cholesky Factorization & VAE Reparameterization Trick
Every PD matrix factors uniquely into $A = L L^T$ ($L$ is lower triangular). To sample correlated Gaussian noise $\mathbf{x} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma)$:
1. Sample standard isotropic Gaussian $\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, I)$.
2. Compute $\mathbf{x} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + L\mathbf{z}$.
3. $\text{Cov}(\mathbf{x}) = L \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{z}\mathbf{z}^T] L^T = L I L^T = L L^T = \Sigma \quad \checkmark$.
